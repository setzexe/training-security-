# Phishing

Phishing is the term to describe **social engineering** with spoofing. It is meant to impersonate something real in order for you to give up information that would be private. This is typically done via email, text, etc. These can look and feel extremely real and remarkable when done well.

It is easy to be fooled by these, but you can easily check. The URL is usually messed up in some capacity. The font, spacing, general format might be weird or outdated. You might be able to click in places that the website typically does not let you.

## Business email compromise

We trust email sources. Again, trust is extremely important in cybersecurity. Attackers take advantage of this trust. 

Spoofed email addresses aree common. In rare cases, attackers find an ability to gain access to a real eamil. Otherwise, they typically use one that looks similar enough. For example...

He11o@gmail.com

Hello@gmail.com

They do not look that different. This gains trust unless that person actively notices the difference. For the actual email, there is an attachment that typically is rather malware or an attempt to steal your information, like your business email. If a business email is compromised, the attacker can send other email's to other user's and also check through any important emails.

## Tricks and misdirection

Attackers do a decent bit to make the victim think that they had gone to a legitimate site. Digital slight of hand if you will. 

Typosquatting is common. This makes the URL of the malicious page look very similar to what we expect. A letter or number off but still looking legitimate enough.

Pretext is also very common. This is just lying to get the victim to spill information. Someone impersonating *Visa* might email them saying they need to check their bills and to log in to the Visa website. This will be a fake website that just sends the information to the attacker. 

Vishing is voice phishing, aka phishing over phone or voicemail. Pretext is the main method of deception for this.

Smishing is SMS phishing. Text message phishing. Spoofing is a problem here too.

It is fine to consider all these in the general "phishing" category.

# Impersonation

This is typically the method to actually phish. Impersonating is just as it sounds; pretending you are someone else in order to get something. There is a trap before an attack and the impersonator is the attack.

They typically will use details relevant to whatever they are impersonating. Someone pretending to be from *Best Buy* could speak as if they understand how geek squad works and this might fool some people. They also can throw a bunch of technical details around that are not really relevant. This can do many things including making the victim think the speaker is legit, make them freaked out cause of the detail, or (and it usually leads to this) feel more enticed to follow along. Along with this, they act as someone with a higher rank than the victim. If they are not speaking in super technical detail, they also can act like buddies to gain your trust and eventually vish you.

While this impersonation happens via vishing (primarily, but it can be any phishing) the attacker is often trying to extract information from you in some capacity. The victim often does not even realize this is happening. These are well documented psychological techniques though, not a blunt "What's your password?"

## Identity Fraud

Attackers can also take advantage of identity fraud if they have decent access to information. They can attack you by opening cards under your name or use your card information. Same logic for banks and loans.

Verification at any step of giving out vital information is essential. Checking who you're giving it to. Also do not just voluntarily give out vital information.
