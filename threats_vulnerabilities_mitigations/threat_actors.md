# Threat Actors

Threat Actors represent the entity responsible for an event that has an impact on the safety of another entity. They basically are the mastermind behind an attack. These are usually the same as the attacker (the person who actually attacks). But in larger scale attacks, this is not always the case. Attacker and threat actor are two distinct roles.

It is useful to know the threat actor in an attack to get a better understanding of why that attack even went down.

## Attributes of Threat Actors

Threat actors can come from anywhere. Maybe internal, maybe outside and trying to get in. This affects another important attribute, their resources / funding. What about their skillset? Are these scripts / tools from an experienced user? Or are they basic automated tools?

Their **motivation** also affects all this. All attacks have a purpose, even if it's boredom / experimentation. This can include:

- Data exfiltration, service disruption, espionage, financial gain
- Blackmail, ethical / philosophical reasons
- Revenge, distruption / chaos

## Types of Attackers

Organizations / threat actors attacking your organization from the outside is usually referred to as **nation states** or **governments**. If these nations have a lot of resources, they can enforce a **APT** (Advanced Persistent Threat) which means constant attacks from different places.

In legitimate government cases; like with the U.S., this applies heavily and has the highest level of sophistication.

**Unskilled Attackers** usually do not have much resources and run pre-made scripts without knowledge of what they're doing. These attackers are usually external, but internal can happen to. These attackers are usually motivated by the hunt itself. Data exfiltration, maybe financials, maybe philosophical.

**Hacktivist**'s are hacker's with a purpose related to activism. These are often also external, but can breach and seem internal. These hackers usually are very sophisticated. Their resources may be limited but they usually have very specific hacking knowledge.

**Insider threat's** are. Insider threat's. They have insider knowledge and usually are motivated by power gain, financial, or revenge. They will use organization resources to screw you over. They may only have intermediate knowledge but they have your resoures. 

**Organized crime** are organized professional criminals that are motivated by money. These are usually external and very sophisticated; you may have one person provided scripts, someone providing resources, someone running these attacks, etc.

**Shadow IT** is moreso internal sabatoge. It might not be malicious, but it might affect your current IT organization. Shadow IT might build their own infrastructure around an existing one, which can put up roadblocks. The budget of this group is typically limited.
