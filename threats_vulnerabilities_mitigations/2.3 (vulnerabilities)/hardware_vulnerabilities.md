# Hardware Vulnerabilities

We are surrounded by hardware devices. These devices themselves are a-whole-nother level of security. Although these don't usually have an accessible operating system, they are still typically accessible by the network. Everything including stuff like light bulbs and garage doors, even refridgerators nowadays via smart features, are connected. **IoT (Internet of Things)** is everywhere, meaning they can connect to a network.

## Firmware

This is typically the term for the software inside of hardware. Imagine your old bank ATM's operating system; you have no idea how that thing is specifically configured. **Vendors / creators** are usually the only people who can fix their firmware whichs fixes the hardware. They're usually the main people to know about problems and care about fixing them. Using a third party vendor to update these could be a risk; do they really know the firmware infrastructure?

## End-of-life

An **EOL (End-of-life)** notice / event is when vendors or developers let their customers know their hardware will stop being sold. While no longer sold, support and security patches / updates might still be implemented. This is important for security and patches on systems who might not have an easy time switching.

## End-of-service-life

**EOSL (End of service life)** is similar but it includes **support and patches** too. Premium cost support may exist, but not often and often not worth it.

## Legacy Platforms

Some devices remain installed for a long time. Perhaps these simply have a specific reason related to the company. This includes applications, OS's, middleware, etc. These often do require extra protection from modern day attacks.

If switching to a modern platform, **assessing the risk** is important too. Perhaps that device is configured in a way to where updating it would brick or fry it.
