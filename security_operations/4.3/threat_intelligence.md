# Threat Intelligence

This refers to the concept of widening your understanding of different threats, different threat actors and why they do it, how to handle these thrreats, etc. This helps one make better security decisions. Researchers, leadership, and security information utilize this information.

## Open-source Intelligence (OSINT)

OSINT is intelligence that is available on the public web. You could find this through the internet, **government data** (public hearngs, reports, etc) and **commercial data** (public databases, maps, etc).

OSINT goes both directions; defenders will use it to learn how to defend against these threats, and attacker will use it to learn how to execute these threats.

## Proprietary and Third-party Intelligence

Gathering information takes time and effort, so companies may just buy it instead. Pre-analyzed cyber data and means of how to stop them for example. Many of these threat intelligence services can come with a plethora of stuff like threat monitoring.

## Information-sharing Organizations

Threat intelligence works best when organizations share it because an attack that hits one company may attack another tomorrow. A problem is that much of this data gets locked away. These organizations fix that by giving people involved structured way of sharing that threat information. CVSS is like this.

## Dark Web Intelligence

The dark web is made up of overlay networks that run on top of the internet. You would need specific configurations to operate this (like the Tor browser). A lot of attackers operate here. You will find alot of detailed information about threats, credentials, etc.

Companies often monitor the dark web in search for anything related to their company. If anything is found, they can prepare for a threat accordingly.
