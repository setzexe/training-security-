# Power resiliency

The foundation of all the work we do and our resources in the power. It is extremely important to properly engineer and prepare for outages.

We usually do not usen our own power. The host being out can cause its customers to go out to. That is why mitigation is far better than attempting to control the power itself. Power could go out for reasons like a car striking a pole, or a hurricane causing power outage for days.

## UPS (Uninterrupted Power Supply)

These are like power bricks that give short term backup power in case of blackouts, surges, etc.

UPS's can scale based on budget and utility. Many UPS's work offline / on standby, simply waiting to be turned on when necessary. On-line / double conversion UPS's are directly on both the powerline and the UPS itself, turning on immediately in case of failure. Line-interactive UPS's are moreso for keeping power proper. If power is underperforming, it might throw in some power to help compensate.

These UPS systems run on batteries that are limited, so size and batteries are a consideration.

## Generators

This is for much longer term power outages. Fuel storage is required, as well as a lot of space typically. But these can power entire systems and buildings. As long as you can fuel the generator (usually with diesel), it will give power. It is recommended to use a UPS with this, as it may take some minutes to get a generator working. The UPS acts as the bridge.
